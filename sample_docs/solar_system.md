# The Solar System — a short reference

## Mercury
Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no
atmosphere, so it cannot trap heat: surface temperatures swing from about 430°C
in daylight to −180°C at night, the largest temperature range of any planet. A
single year on Mercury lasts just 88 Earth days. It has no moons.

## Venus
Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System, with a surface temperature
around 465°C — hotter even than Mercury — because its thick carbon-dioxide
atmosphere creates a runaway greenhouse effect. It rotates backwards compared to
most planets and spins so slowly that a Venusian day is longer than its year.

## Earth
Earth is the only planet known to support life and the only one with liquid
water covering most of its surface. Its atmosphere is roughly 78% nitrogen and
21% oxygen. Earth has a single large Moon, which stabilises the planet's tilt
and drives the ocean tides.

## Mars
Mars, the "Red Planet", gets its colour from iron-oxide (rust) dust. It hosts
Olympus Mons, the tallest volcano in the Solar System at about 22 km high, and
Valles Marineris, a canyon system over 4,000 km long. Mars has two small moons,
Phobos and Deimos, and is the main target of current rover exploration.

## Jupiter
Jupiter is the largest planet — so massive it could contain all the other
planets combined. It is a gas giant famous for the Great Red Spot, a storm
larger than Earth that has raged for centuries. Jupiter has the most known moons
of any planet, including the four large Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede,
and Callisto.

## Saturn
Saturn is best known for its spectacular ring system, made mostly of billions of
chunks of water ice mixed with rock and dust. It is the least dense planet —
less dense than water — so it would float given a large enough ocean. Its moon
Titan is larger than the planet Mercury and has a thick atmosphere.

## Uranus
Uranus is an ice giant that rotates on its side, with an axial tilt of about 98
degrees, likely the result of an ancient collision. This sideways spin gives it
extreme seasons lasting roughly 21 Earth years each. It appears pale blue-green
because methane in its atmosphere absorbs red light.

## Neptune
Neptune is the most distant planet from the Sun and the windiest, with storms
driving winds up to about 2,100 km/h — the fastest in the Solar System. It is a
deep blue ice giant and was the first planet discovered by mathematical
prediction rather than direct observation.
